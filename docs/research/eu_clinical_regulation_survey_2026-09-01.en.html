2The regulatory map — and the mistake everyone makes

The single most common error in this field is a scope mix-up. Annex 11 and Annex 22 are manufacturing (GMP) documents. They do not govern clinical trials. Selling “Annex 11 compliance” to a trial sponsor is a category error.

Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 — binding law Art. 56 data recorded so it can be “accurately reported, interpreted and verified” · Art. 57 TMF complete “at all times” · Art. 58 25-year archive; “any alteration to the content of the clinical trial master file shall be traceable”
↓ Art. 47
ICH E6(R3) — in force in the EU 2025-07-23 (Annex 2: 2027-01-15) New standalone §4 “Data governance”: audit trails must not be disabled, UTC timestamps unambiguous, audit-trail review must be a planned, risk-based activity
↓ what inspectors actually inspect against
EMA/INS/GCP/112288/2023 — computerised systems, in effect 2023-09-10 ALCOA++ codified at §4.5 · audit trail §6.2.1 · audit-trail review §6.2.2 · certified copies §6.5 · migration “e.g. checksum” §6.10 · control of data §6.6 · AI explicitly out of scope
Separate stack — manufacturing only, all still draft

EudraLex Vol. 4 Annex 11 · Annex 22 (AI) · Chapter 4. The Annex 11 revision contains zero occurrences of AI/ML/model — AI was deliberately split into Annex 22 so it could be revised independently. Annex 22 has no finalisation date, and EMA is reconsidering its central exclusion (a blanket ban on generative AI in critical GMP applications).

3The one hook that names the technology

Section 6.6 of the EMA guideline was read directly from the source PDF. The word ledger appears exactly once in the 52-page document, here:

The sponsor should not have exclusive control of the data entered in a computerised system at any point in time. All data held by the sponsor that has been generated in a clinical trial should be verifiable to a copy of these data that is not held (or that has not been held) by the sponsor.

[…] Instead of a system maintained by an independent service provider, the sponsor may take other adequate technical measures that preclude sole control. E.g. the verifiability of data (transactions) by an independent (distributed) tamper-proof ledger may provide comparable security to a system maintained by an independent service provider. This should be justified and documented.

EMA/INS/GCP/112288/2023 §6.6, p. 22/52 — in effect since 10 September 2023
What the ledger does Discharges independence — precludes sole sponsor control. It is a substitute for “use an independent service provider”.
What it does not do It is not a substitute for integrity, for the audit trail, or for retention.
The price “This should be justified and documented.” The justification document is itself a required deliverable — and therefore a product.

The mandate arrived, and it did not select for chains. This does not contradict §6.6 — the two operate at different levels. §6.6 grants permission; DSCSA measures demand. Use permission to defend; never to forecast.

5The obligation with no method — where the opening is

“…where the processing is an integral part of the solution […] It should be possible to validate the correct operation of the processing.”

EMA/INS/GCP/112288/2023 §4.4

No EU instrument states how to validate a bioinformatics pipeline. Not the CTR, not ICH E6(R3), not the computerised-systems guideline. The nearest text — EMA/CHMP/718998/2016 on good pharmacogenomic practice (in effect 2018-09-01) — walks the whole NGS workflow and then says only “use validated bioinformatics methods and algorithms”, delegating quality assurance to ISO 15189 or CAP/CLIA. It is a scientific guideline with no computerised-systems apparatus.

7Counter-evidence — the section to read before the pitch deck

Everything here will be raised by a jury, a pharma QA head, or a competitor. Raise it first.

Counter-evidenceDetail
No regulator asks for cryptographic proof Term counts across 21 CFR Part 11, Annex 11, the EMA guideline, ICH E6(R3), Reg. 536/2014 and FDA’s 2024 Q&A: cryptograph / hash / blockchain / immutable ≈ 0 throughout. The one exception is §6.6.
Part 11 is not enforced FDA’s 2003 Scope and Application guidance: “we do not intend to take enforcement action… [on] the validation, audit trail, record retention, and record copying requirements”. Part 11 cited 0 times in any programme area, FY2021–FY2025.
The regulator narrows the scope you would automate MHRA: “It is not necessary for audit trail review to include every system activity.” Where the functionality does not exist, an acceptable control is “an SOP, and use of log books.”
Wrong end of the citation distribution FY2025 FDA BIMO citations: 56.3% land on investigator sites, 4.5% on sponsors. The party being cited is not the party with the budget.
Verification already costs a fortune and finds nothing I-SPY COVID: US$6.1M and 61,073 person-hours changed 0.36% of fields, zero primary-outcome impact. Best cost-of-pain slide and the strongest argument that marginal assurance has low yield.
The customer published the answer, and it is free Thirteen accredited Nordic clinical-bioinformatics units, 2025: “Data integrity must be verified using file hashing.” Plus 156 nf-core pipelines, Galaxy (free, 400k+ users), RO-Crate, and RFC 3161 — Standards Track since August 2001.
Wüst & Gervais Single writer + an always-online trusted third party → the decision flowchart terminates at “Don’t use blockchain.” A single lab writing its own attestations fails this test.
No budget line exists The HHS/ASPE clinical-trial cost decomposition names eleven components and contains no software or data-integrity line at all.
Veeva already owns “inspection ready” FY2026 10-K, 1,552 customers, US$3.195bn revenue, treats audit trails and Part 11 as baseline. Occurrences of “blockchain”: zero.
Systematic review 10 of 10 clinical-trial blockchain systems were “prototypes or simulations, and none were tested in real-world regulatory settings”.

The question the first paragraph must answer

What does this do that sha256sum + an RFC 3161 timestamp + an RO-Crate does not — for a customer whose own professional consensus document already says hashing is sufficient, and whose regulator has never once asked for more?

The answer: what is sold is the verdict, not the record.

A hash says a file did not change. It cannot say:

1 · Can this even be re-run? gc_codescan grades the pinning strength of the declared dependency closure — env_capture_class A/B/C. A hash does not grade.
2 · Is there anything to compare against? gc_tier2_execute reports no_target_declared as a complete result. A hash cannot say “there is no target”.
3 · Who judged? Tier-2 re-executes on a third party’s infrastructure. A hash is compatible with pure self-report.

This also fixes the buyer problem: a verdict is bought by quality assurance; a record platform is bought by IT.

9TEE — what may be claimed, in three tiers

The security record got materially worse in the last twelve months. The honest position separates three adversaries.

✓ can claim
(a) Remote software adversary. TDX and SEV-SNP hold up reasonably. This is the defensible claim.
▲ weakened
(b) Malicious cloud operator. This is what TEEs are sold on, and it has been broken in software repeatedly.
✕ no claim exists
(c) Adversary with physical access. Intel and AMD both state interposer attacks are out of scope with no mitigations planned. An excluded threat, not a residual risk.

The sentence you inherit

“This ran in a TEE” becomes “someone holding a valid key asserted that this ran in a TEE.” For regulated evidence those are different sentences, and only the second is true.

But only one of the two uses is damaged

(A) Proving to a third party

“This computation ran unmodified.” Damaged — quotes are forgeable for $1,000.

(B) Confidentiality from the operator

Computing over a customer’s closed data and configuration without being able to read them. Intact against a remote software adversary — and there is no alternative at genomic scale (ZK is 10⁴–10⁶× short).

The design consequence: BSI C5:2026 OPS-33.01AC grades who verifies

very strong
Customer retrieves the evidence and verifies it in an environment they fully trust
very strong
Provider verifies and returns the result and the evidence; customer re-verifies in its own environment
strong
Customer retrieves the evidence and sends it to a verification service it trusts
weak
Provider retrieves, verifies in a service under its own control, and returns only the result

One line of architecture follows: hand over the evidence, not the result — and let the other party verify it in their own environment. The same rule appears independently in KairosChain’s own INV-5 (“the deciding context must never author what judges it”).

The objection to prepare for

Every authority that has looked at genomics specifically — NIST, the Swiss FDPIC, CNIL’s LINC, the EDPB — named homomorphic encryption and secure multi-party computation, not TEEs. In Switzerland this is concrete: SPHN has already committed its federated-query layer to Tune Insight (HE + MPC + k-anonymity). “TEEs are faster” is not an answer.

The existence proof, and the argument template

Edgeless Systems × gematik ePA — Germany’s national electronic patient record, Intel confidential computing, scaled for up to 50 million insured, mandatory for medical facilities since 2025-10-01. The driver was a regulatory Betreiberausschluss — operator exclusion. That is precisely §6.6’s logic in another jurisdiction. No genomic data.

Meanwhile in genomics: zero TDX papers, zero SEV-SNP papers, zero TEE variant-calling or alignment papers at whole-genome scale. Decentriq (Zurich) has real health customers and zero genomics.

10Data protection — the section that fixes the architecture

EDPB Guidelines 02/2025 on blockchain reached final v2.0 on 2026-07-07, replacing the CNIL 2018 note and materially tightening it.

ClauseWhat it says
§104Personal data on-chain in clear text, encrypted or hashed form is “not advisable”. Store off-chain.
§52The hash is also personal data. “Unsalted or unkeyed hashes should, in general, not be considered sufficient” — a reversal of CNIL 2018.
§53–54On-chain only as “proof of existence” — a pointer, a cryptographic commitment, or a keyed hash. §53 prefers perfectly-hiding commitments.
§50“Technical impossibility cannot be invoked to justify non-compliance.”
§40“Organisations should favour permissioned blockchains.”
§13Chains generated and controlled by and for a single entity are out of scope for part of the guidelines.
Rec. 9Consent should not be the legal basis where the architecture cannot delete the parties’ personal data.

Spain’s AEPD proof-of-concept implements on-chain erasure by a coordinated hard fork. Regulators treat chains as rewritable. Immutability is not a shield; it is a design choice you will be asked to justify.

Two hits on the selective-disclosure pitch

1 · “We never see the data” does not make you a processor. Guidelines 1/2026 §135: an entity can be a controller “even though it does not process personal data at all… or only processes pseudonymised data”. Roles turn on the governance mechanism.

2 · Genomic data cannot be argued into anonymity. Guidelines 1/2026 §165: genetic profiles are personal data by default, anonymous only in exceptional circumstances; §167 extends the risk to blood relatives, permanently. CNIL fined IQVIA €5,000,000 (2026-05-26) running a weaker version of that argument.

Resulting architecture

On-chainKeyed commitments only; perfectly-hiding preferred. Unsalted, unkeyed hashes: not permitted.
Off-chainAll personal data. Documented key-destruction procedure with test evidence.
Chain typePermissioned · known participants · nodes resident in the EEA / CH.
OperatorStart single-operator (§13 exposure is smaller). When it goes multi-party, design the Art. 26 joint-controller arrangement in, not after.
ConsentNever make the consent record itself the ledger’s purpose (Rec. 9). Record only when an effect began.
SealingeIDAS qualified timestamp — it carries a statutory legal presumption that a blockchain anchor does not.

Note the convergence: the on-chain form the EDPB prefers — a cryptographic commitment — is exactly the primitive a selective-disclosure protocol needs anyway. The constraint and the design want the same thing.

Two hazards

“Mutual attestation” is an occupied term. All ten arXiv hits mean two enclaves verifying each other’s code measurements. A security reviewer will mis-file the work on the title alone. Rename.

The five-dimensional trust-scoring model is already attacked — Hu, Rong & Van Kleek, FAccT 2026 (arXiv:2605.30169) argue agents are “ontologically dissociative” and that reputation-style governance is “structurally inapplicable”. Drop it or defend it explicitly.

What is left — narrow, but real

1 · Verifier independence as a carried, checkable property

“B was not authored, configured or controlled by A” — as an invariant carried in the evidence, rather than assumed by deployment. SLSA VSA conspicuously does not require this.

2 · The domain binding

Nothing cryptographically attests a BioCompute Object (IEEE 2791). The verification kit and the FDA concordance workflow are procedural and manual. No SCITT document mentions healthcare, clinical trials or genomics at all.

Honest split: the protocol is not novel; the independence invariant is thin but real; the domain binding is the substantial part.

12The business shape

Customer side Runs the computation · self-declares · issues a KairosChain attestation over its own claim
Our side Re-executes inside a TEE · compares the output · hands back the evidence, not just the result
↓ compose the two attestations
Output for a third party (regulator, partner, notified body) Not a “certificate” — no such instrument exists in regulation. What exists: validation documentation, audit-trail-review records, certified copies, qualification dossiers. Build evidence a sponsor can put in its own dossier and defend.
Graded, because re-execution is not always possible

Controlled-access data often cannot be shared. gc_tier1_assess (claims) → gc_codescan (pinning class) → gc_tier2_execute (execution). The ladder itself is the product.

Document generation is the most regulation-backed part

EMA §6.6 “justified and documented” · EDPB blockchain Recommendation 1 “written rationale, alternatives considered” · BSI C5 OPS-32.01AC eight-point framework. Three instruments explicitly require a document.

“What you cannot prove” is also the product

More defensible than “we certify you”, and it generates the next engagement. Tier-2 reporting no_target_declared as a complete result already embodies this.

Getting past procurement

Selling into pharma runs 6–18 months, with vendor qualification alone taking 2–4 months and security questionnaires “often 200+ questions”. The named failure mode in the trade press: startups run out of money before the purchasing cycle completes. Two documented ways around it:

(a) Sell the service, year one Work as a consultant under the sponsor’s own quality system → vendor qualification is never triggered.
(b) Sell through a qualified party, later A CRO or consultancy resells the output under its existing qualification → no 200-question questionnaire per logo.
